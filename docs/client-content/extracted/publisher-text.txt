PURPOSEWhy would someone choose to do business with us?Or, conversely, why would someone choose not to do business with us?“People do not buy what you do, they buy why you do it.” - Simon SinekIn business, people tend not to ask why because they assume they know the answer or they should know the answer.  Instead of asking or clarifying why, most people make decisions based on what they think they know.  Which is a mistake.  So, why does The Howell Group exist?Our purpose is to deliver our client’s mission through the development of people-centric teams and companies devoted to this purpose.  We continually ask ourselves; what does it take to ensure a successful implementation of our client’s mission?  Is it technology?  Is it a procedural set of processes?  Is it a group of people and companies with passion and integrity?  The answer is yes!  Yes to every one of those questions.  But there is more, and through many years of implementing different technologies and different contractual process on every type of project possible we have learned by asking questions, listening to the answers and observing behavior.This requires us to rely on our ability to be intuitive, to recognize problems and successes, to understand people and to create specific solutions.  These ideas not only need to be functional, but must also connect on an emotional level because we genuinely care about every person with which we come in contact.“The complacent company is a dead company.  Success today requires the ability and drive to constantly rethink, reinvigorate, react and reinvent.” - Bill GatesOnly companies that act like commodities are the ones who wake up every morning with the challenge of how to differentiate themselves.  We have a clear sense of our purpose and therefore, we do not think of ourselves as being like everyone else.  We are not concerned about how we compare to anyone else because we do not see it as a competition.  We also understand that real achievement does not happen overnight, which is why we are simply and obsessively focused on being better than we were yesterday.

PROCESSESTypically, the overall life-cycle process of a project is dysfunctional via silos and poor communication, which leads to adversarial relationships.  Over the last ten years, while the design and construction industries have made a number of improvements, they have been slow to adopt new technologies and processes.  Regardless of the stage or phase of a project, we develop and implement a specific and unified process throughout.What if each of these processes could be improved by 1%-5%?
Questions to consider:Who is responsible for the overall project from beginning to end?Are the right people in the right positions?Are the right people making the right decisions?Do the right people have the necessary information to make the right decisions?Are the current processes efficient and effective?How is communication achieved between all of the various parties?What are the “known” and “unknown” interests / motivations of each group?What are the defined objectives and expectations of each group?	Does everyone know?	Does everyone agree?DBIA Best Practices:Critical questions for establishing a project strategyWhat is the most important project objective?What is the biggest project concern / challenge?Efficient involvement of key stakeholders (e.g., ultimate users)?Ability to delegate authority to make final decisions promptly?Rules governing procurement?Owner’s dominant personality and culture?

PROCESSESAn Owner’s choice of project delivery system and procurement approach strongly influence project results.  These choices are among the first decisions an Owner makes on a project, and they form the foundation of how the project will be developed, procured and executed, and how the key stakeholders communicate and relate to each other.  In making these choices, it is critical for an Owner to consider the particulars and circumstances of each project, including the procurement options available to the Owner.  After thoroughly considering these issues an Owner should make a strategic decision as to how to take full advantage of the many benefits that are inherent with each delivery system.An Owner should conduct a proactive and objective assessment of the unique characteristics of its program / project and its organization before deciding on a particular delivery system.An Owner should implement a procurement plan that enhances collaboration and other benefits of the delivery system and is in harmony with the reasons that the Owner chose the delivery system.An Owner using a competitive procurement that seeks price and technical proposal should: establish clear evaluation and processes; ensure that the process is fair, open and transparent; and value both technical and price in the selection process.All team members should be educated and trained in the chosen delivery process and be knowledgeable of the differences between it and other delivery systems.The project team should establish logistics and infrastructure to support the project delivery.The project team, at the outset of the project, should establish processes to facilitate timely and effective communication, collaboration and issue resolution.The project team should focus on the design management and commissioning / turnover process and ensure that there is alignment among the team as to how to execute these processes.ISSUE RESOLUTION PROCESS

SERVICESProgram Management.  Is the process of managing several related projects in a coordinated manner with oversight of, and focus on the client’s purpose.  A Program Manager uses this oversight to support project-level activity and to ensure the client’s goals are met by providing decision-making capacity that cannot be achieved at an individual project level.  Understanding how our clients work allows us to have insight into how decisions are made.Being capable of looking at the bigger picture allows us to identify and describe a specific delivery method regarding how projects are delivered in a building or on a campus.Design Management.  Is the process of managing the development of a project’s design.  The objective of design management is to develop and maintain an efficient team in which it can achieve the project goals through design.  Design Management is a comprehensive activity at all levels of the team, from discovery to execution with a focus on human-centered solutions.Construction Management.  Is a service that provides our clients with effective management of a project’s scope, quality, schedule, cost, safety and function. Regardless of which role we occupy, we represent the Owner’s interest and provide oversight over the entire program or project directly for the Owner.We are able to bring our extensive combined experience from many years of working at various organizations to create an environment encompassing all of the great things we have learned while maintaining a client-centric culture.Our success is based on a defined scope of work and goals for the entire team, with a high level of constant communication and total collaboration.
